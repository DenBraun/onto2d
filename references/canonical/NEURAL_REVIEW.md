# Neural evidence

The current [study records](neural-review.json) identify preparations, observed
variables, findings, reading limits and competing interpretations. Claims and
relations are defined in [graph.json](graph.json).

Neural development, synaptic induction, rhythms, task behavior, affect-related
assays, neuromodulation, sampled activity, consciousness readouts, sleep and
ensembles require distinct evidence. An observed association does not become
a neural intervention; a task response does not uniquely identify a mechanism
or subjective experience. Null, conditional and positive outcomes remain separate.

## Action responses and empathy

[Gallese et al. (1996)](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/119.2.593) reports
observation/execution responses in 92 of 532 sampled F5 units from two macaques.
The graph distinguishes these units from observation-only responses, response
congruence and the 14-unit light/dark subset. Separate EMG sessions and 49 F1
control units retain their own contexts. Table 2's rows and Results give 25
observation-only units, while its printed total is 92; this discrepancy remains
an explicit source limitation.

Motor matching as a recognition mechanism remains a hypothesis. The
[Rizzolatti and Craighero review](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.neuro.27.070203.144230)
provides theoretical context for action understanding, imitation and language.
The [Decety and Jackson account](https://doi.org/10.1177/1534582304267187) supplies
an attributed empathy definition and proposed functional architecture. Neither
review demonstrates a universal minimal social circuit. Action observation,
felt sharing, self-other distinction and helping need separate outcome measures.

The complete Decety and Jackson scientific text and all six figures are reviewed.
The authors explicitly describe their psychological constructs as nonprimitive
and distinguish executive functions from mental-state attribution. Perception,
general affect and working-memory maintenance therefore have separate unresolved
necessity questions. Their review includes observed, imagined and narrated
situations, different lesion tasks and alternative interpretations of frontal
activity. These secondary summaries do not supply one measured universal circuit.

Measurement identity is explicit in the graph. Box 1 calls a 33-item 1972
instrument Balanced Emotional Empathy, while the
[scale author's description](https://www.kaaj.com/psych/scales/emp.html)
identifies a distinct 30-item BEES and its 1996 manual. A scale name cannot merge
item counts, scoring or validation across versions. The manual and validation
data remain unread. Self-report, recognition, physiological response,
interpersonal accuracy and helping require distinct outcome definitions.

[Gallese's Roots of Empathy](https://doi.org/10.1159/000072786) and
[Manifold Nature of Interpersonal Relations](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2002.1234)
are distinct 2003 publications. The first is read in full; the second has reviewed
passages on pages 517-518 and 524-525. The original card's abbreviated citation
does not uniquely identify either work. Their shared-manifold proposal is an
explicit hypothesis in the graph. Its three descriptive levels do not determine
geometric dimension, a neural sequence or a carrier minimum. The first paper
states that systematic testing had not occurred at publication; this is not a
claim about all subsequent research. Common-mechanism and geometric-construction
questions require separate discriminating tests.

[Mukamel et al. (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2010.02.045) records
human action responses in clinically selected medial frontal and temporal regions.
Its 1177 units include 665 single and 512 multiunit recordings. The graph keeps
the 90 matching units distinct from the detailed 68-unit subset, its response
directions and its effector-selectivity classification. The
[supplement](https://ars.els-cdn.com/content/image/1-s2.0-S0960982210002332-mmc1.pdf)
identifies five auditory-cued and sixteen word-cued participants; pooled results
do not establish equivalent protocols. Its latency measure is time to a response
extremum, not neural onset. Table components, one chi-square/P pair, simulated
intervals and normalization wording require resolution before exact numerical
use. Motor suppression and agency remain hypotheses.

[Singer et al. (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1093535) scans the female
partner in each of sixteen couples during cued self/partner stimulation. Shared
BOLD locations, stronger self-pain sensory contrasts and trait-score correlations
are separate observations. Spatial overlap does not identify common neurons or
common coding; a nonsignificant rating contrast does not establish equal
experience. Main text and all four figures are read, but the supporting methods
remain unread. Pain-specific affective representation is an explicit hypothesis
requiring matched alternatives. Neither study supplies calibrated dependencies
from generic perception, affect or working memory to a universal empathy node.

### Intracranial pain-picture recordings

[Tan et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-49541-1) contributes
clinical iEEG observations, a sixteen-person rating subset and a computational
decoder context. The 297 channels come from twenty-two patients with overlapping
regional samples. Channel pairs share patients and electrodes; the 72
ACC-amygdala pairs come from four patients. Separate healthy controls supply
behavioral ratings only. These preparations do not identify a complete empathy
circuit or a working-memory prerequisite.

Regional power, thresholded detection times, power correlation, transfer entropy
and phase-amplitude coupling retain distinct observables. Directional statistical
predictability and asymmetric phase/amplitude assignment do not establish a
causal neural route. Nearest-white-matter referencing fails to retain some effects
at the original threshold. With 50 ms smoothing, the early IFG cluster has
P=0.029; its detection therefore depends on accepting a threshold above 0.01.
The proposed neurodynamic organization remains a hypothesis.

The decoder reports 76.28 percent accuracy with thirteen features and 49.78
percent after ordered removal of eight. This path does not prove a unique
minimum feature set, much less eight necessary physical components. The
[published helper code](https://github.com/Huixin-Tan/iEEG_empathy/tree/f96ef160130fcd2c31d82fc065dc67687188f616)
uses training-fold scaling and absolute SVM coefficients. These coefficients are
not graph dependency weights. Channel/pair splits and trial cross-validation
do not establish participant-disjoint or new-stimulus-family generalization.

Selected saved arrays confirm the removal endpoint of 49.775 percent; reading
them does not reproduce training. The S18 source arrays and patient-count table
contain 254 amygdala-IFG pairs, while its caption prints 259. That discrepancy,
same-sample sensitivity analyses and incomplete pipeline availability remain
explicit source limits. Perceived pain, own unpleasantness and empathy ratings
are different endpoints; a power-arousal correlation null does not establish
pain specificity against matched nonpain aversive stimuli.

## Signal propagation

[Randi et al. (2023)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10632145/) reports
downstream calcium responses to targeted stimulation in immobilized TWISP
nematodes. The graph represents three named effects: AVJR to AVDR, AVER to AVAR,
and SAADL to OLLR. These effective responses do not identify a unique direct
synapse or demonstrate a universal cognitive architecture.

Unmeasured pairs, nonsignificant responses and equivalence-tested nonconnections
are different data states. Existing Structural Geometry processed-export
analyses use a different aggregation; they do not independently reproduce the
publication's response classifier or q-values.

Shannon's two-part 1948 communication paper supplies a formal framework.
Mapping it to biological variables and a measured task remains an explicit
modeling question. Its two installment identifiers are recorded with the
combined [reprint](data/shannon1948.pdf); neither is assigned to the entire work.

## Glial experiments

[Mu et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2019.05.050) links selected
radial-astrocyte interventions to calcium and swimming readouts in larval
zebrafish. Partial ablation reduces futility-induced passivity; activation can
suppress swimming. The graph keeps intact and ablated conditions, free-swimming
controls and probabilistic-feedback responses distinct. Approximately half the
fish failed the behavioral inclusion criterion. Fictive swim signals are motor
commands in restrained, paralyzed animals. The evidence opposes a universal
exclusion of glia from task-related information processing; a unique accumulator
algorithm and its molecular threshold remain unresolved.

[Agulhon et al. (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1184821) reports null
transmission/plasticity results in mouse SC-CA1 slices under specified astrocyte
calcium manipulations. [de Ceglia et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06502-w)
reports selected astrocyte glutamate responses and reduced DG potentiation after
VGLUT1 deletion. These are different regions, ages, interventions and protocols.
The paired LTP source table contains 16 pairs; it does not identify their nesting
within the 12 reported mice. The glutamate-imaging table cannot resolve the
conflicting total cell counts, so exact in situ response proportions remain
unadmitted. Human transcript signatures are observational.

The [Araque et al. review](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2014.02.007) supplies
context for preparation-dependent gliotransmission. Fields' book and the fifth
edition of *Principles of Neural Science* have bibliographic records with unread
chapter limits. Neither metadata nor these astrocyte experiments establishes
one general glial property, universal carrier counts, or necessary dependencies
on synaptogenesis, neuromodulation and whole-organ coordination.

[Djukic et al. (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0723-07.2007) reports
reduced potassium- and glutamate-associated uptake currents after developmental
Kir4.1 deletion. The targeted lineage includes astrocytes and oligodendrocytes.
The P15-20 voltage-clamp assay measures pharmacologically subtracted currents;
it does not directly measure extracellular concentrations. Separate P20-25
field recordings show increased early potentiation, with no significant late
LTP difference. Potassium-only mediation is not identified.

[Suzuki et al. (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2011.02.018) reports
impaired inhibitory-avoidance retention after hippocampal MCT4 or MCT2 antisense.
L-lactate rescues the MCT4 condition; Figure 6 and its Results report no MCT2
rescue despite a conflicting Discussion sentence. Regional transporter
knockdown is not a cell-restricted intervention or direct substrate-flux assay.
Retention latency, short-term nulls and dose-specific rescue conditions remain
separate. Full supplementary animal/task methods and numerical tables are unread.

[Etxeberria et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0908-16.2016)
reports more oligodendrocytes, shorter myelin internodes and slower compound
action potentials after developmental visual deprivation. These are separate
cell-count, transgenic-labeling and isolated-nerve assays. The graph does not
turn their common intervention into a directly established internode-to-speed
relation. Text/caption conflicts concern internode medians, genetic-assay
labels, myelinated fractions and the conduction summary; numerical calibration
requires resolution and animal-identified raw data.

[Schafer et al. (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2012.03.026) reports
activity-sensitive engulfment of retinal inputs by developing microglia.
Developmental CR3/CD11b and C3 knockouts reduce engulfment; CR3 loss increases
eye-specific projection overlap and structural synaptic puncta in separate age
cohorts. These anatomical endpoints do not measure electrical efficacy. Local
CR3 expression supports microglial attribution, while the knockout itself is
not an inducible, cell-restricted manipulation. The exact sequence of activity,
complement tagging and removal, adult maintenance and immune surveillance
require separate evidence. Complete supplementary methods remain unread.

[Nimmerjahn et al. (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1110647) observes
continuous microglial process remodeling in adult mouse neocortex and directed
process recruitment after targeted capillary injury. Somata did not migrate
toward the lesion during the reported observation window. These are distinct
morphological readouts. Projected tissue coverage and protective surveillance
remain interpretations without direct clearance or neuronal-survival assays.
Supporting animal and imaging methods remain unread; inconsistent inclusion
counts and diameter summaries prevent quantitative calibration.

[Badimon et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-2777-8) supplies
separate neonatal culture, adult cortical imaging, striatal calcium,
microdialysis, tissue-fluorescence and seizure-challenge experiments. The graph
keeps PLX5622 treatment, CD39 deletion, whole-animal P2ry12 loss, neuronal A1
receptor loss and neuronal Il34 deletion distinct. A1 agonist rescue in the
CD39 and Il34 preparations does not transfer to mice lacking the relevant
neuronal receptor. Calcium and challenge-specific nulls remain explicit.

The unchanged [Figure 4 workbook](data/41586_2020_2777_MOESM11_ESM.xlsx)
provides exact cited cell ranges. Figure 4c contains extracellular dialysate
concentrations under anesthesia; Figure 4h contains tissue-assay fluorescence,
which cannot inherit concentration units. Culture wells do not establish
independent donor replication, and imaging tips and cells are nested within
mice. Conflicting age ranges, CNO routes, bouton-region definitions, injection
units and co-treatment sample counts remain unresolved. These experiments
constrain a purinergic feedback hypothesis without identifying one quantitative
mechanism across all preparations. Raw imaging and code have not been replayed.

## Activity and gene regulation

[Su et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4494) reports bulk dentate-gyrus
accessibility and RNA responses after ECS in adult male mice. c-Fos knockdown
attenuates selected responses; overexpression without ECS partially mimics
opening and associated RNA increases. The graph keeps these interventions and
assays separate. External ChIP-seq annotations and independent ATAC/RNA samples
do not establish an enhancer-to-transcript causal link within the same cell.

The unchanged [region workbook](data/41593_2017_BFnn4494_MOESM61_ESM.xlsx)
provides the exact genomic rows: 11,438/1,739 early open/closed regions,
4,175/484 shared through four hours and 597/22 through 24 hours. These are
cross-sectional region-list counts, not longitudinal cell measurements or
independent peak-calling reproduction. The 24-hour sheets have conflicting
titles, and one repeats a header. The [expression workbook](data/41593_2017_BFnn4494_MOESM62_ESM.xlsx)
retains nonfinite values and Excel errors. Its finite-value subset supports
114 shared early upregulated test IDs, but the full counts remain unresolved;
Figure 6c says 144 and the main text says 114.

Low late c-Fos protein and nonsignificant selected-locus ChIP are observations.
They do not replace a selective maintenance perturbation. Figure 7c's caption
also conflicts with the plot and methods on timepoints. The graph does not
claim 24-hour ChIP, lifelong persistence, physiological learning or heritable
memory. Primer discrepancies, lack of randomization/blinding and incomplete
sequencing replay limit quantitative interpretation.

[Weaver et al. (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1276) reports maternal-care
associations and a cross-fostering effect on the rat GR exon 1_7 promoter's
CpG site 16. Same-class foster controls were other mothers of that class.
Neighboring site 17 has a cross-fostering null. Separate developmental cohorts
do not follow individual cells over time, and bulk hippocampus does not isolate
neurons. Cross-fostering changes a care environment, not one measured neural
state or a single maternal behavior.

Adult TSA experiments provide separate ChIP, bisulfite, GR-protein and
corticosterone outcomes. TSA affects site 17 in both maternal groups, so its
methylation pattern is not identical to the maternal-care effect. The stress
cohort is separate from the protein cohort; high-care offspring have a reported
stress-response null. The broad drug intervention does not identify a complete
chromatin-to-endocrine mediation chain.

The publisher's July 2004 correction supplies corrected labels, corticosterone
units and bisulfite-PCR annealing temperature. Sample counts, adult ages,
some protocol units and Figure 2-associated t/P pairs still conflict across
the article and supplement. The graph retains those limits rather than a
recomputed confidence claim. Sweatt's perspective, the two identified Meaney
and Szyf reviews, and Noble's book metadata provide conceptual context only.
Physiological persistence and general downward causation remain hypotheses.

[Marco et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-020-00717-0) supplies a
five-day conditioning comparison. A changed environment yields fewer Arc/eYFP
co-labeled cells and less freezing than the training environment; persistent
eYFP counts have a reported null. Figure 1f measures DG cells per slice despite
its broader caption. Section-level statistics and unclear freezing denominators
prevent an independent animal-level confidence estimate.

The molecular profiles concern separately sampled, marker-selected populations.
Basal means post-training label-negative nuclei, not untrained animals. Shared
accessibility across Early, Late and Reactivated samples does not follow the
same cells. Whole-genome Hi-C covers Basal, Early and Late; targeted promoter
capture covers four populations. Contact frequencies constrain structural
interpretations without identifying unique spatial coordinates. These records
remain observational, and the chromatin-priming mechanism remains a hypothesis.

The unchanged [RNA workbook](data/41593_2020_717_MOESM9_ESM.xlsx) contains
88, 974 and 516 gene rows in its three comparisons. The first and third sheets
have opposite signs for fold change and the reported Wald statistic; the
published software lacks the contrast/export code needed to establish the
direction convention. One third-sheet row falls just below the plotted absolute
log2-fold-change threshold; excluding it would reconcile the figure's total
of 515, but source cells remain intact. These observations do not constitute
RNA-seq reproduction or justify signed gene-specific graph relations.

The reporting summary's exclusions do not reconcile the ATAC library inventory;
Hi-C pooling counts also conflict with Methods, and one Early library was
excluded. Selected histone ChIP assays retain locus-specific nulls. eIF4E and
GRIA1 puncta are protein signals, not translation rates or synaptic currents;
axonal/dendritic shaft labels conflict across Methods, image and caption.
Selective chromatin perturbation and matched behavioral outcomes are still
needed to test necessity or a complete molecular mediation chain.

Source reading, independent reproduction and discriminating experiments remain
open where the individual records say so. Upcoming scientific questions are in
[NEURAL_NEXT_REVIEW.md](NEURAL_NEXT_REVIEW.md).

## Neuroimmune experiments

[Jin et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07469-y) provides
separate mouse preparations for peripheral challenge, neural manipulation,
circulating cytokines, endotoxin survival and live bacterial infection. The graph
keeps Fos-based TRAP2, Dbh-cre, Trpa1-cre and Calca-cre populations distinct.
FOS and calcium are different readouts. Vagotomy reduces the measured cNST
calcium response with residual signal; it does not establish removal of every
immune-to-brain route. Injected and intestinally perfused cytokines have separate
contexts and do not establish direct neuronal receptor binding.

Calca-cre activation reduces IL-6 and IL-1beta but has an IL-10 null. Its profile
cannot inherit the Trpa1-cre IL-10 effect. Saline, inhibitory-neuron and
corticosterone nulls also retain their assay limits. Transcript clusters and
retrograde labels are observations, without inferred functional weights or
anatomical coordinates. Reported stimulus overlap prevents a universal claim
of exclusive neuronal subtypes.

Activation improves survival after high-dose LPS but increases tissue bacterial
load under a different, sustained-activation Salmonella protocol. These outcomes
have different exposures and endpoints. Lower inflammatory cytokines therefore
do not establish uniformly improved host defense. The descending effector route
remains unresolved.

The [reporting summary](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-024-07469-y/MediaObjects/41586_2024_7469_MOESM1_ESM.pdf)
distinguishes stimulus ordering from animal allocation. Animal allocation was
not randomized, investigators were not blinded, and raw data and custom code
have not been obtained or replayed. Exact acquisition sequences, exclusion
counts, tracing labels and matched CNO exposure in the DSS assay need resolution.
The DSS result has no active graph relation.

The [Dantzer review](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn2297),
[Tracey review](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature01321) and
[Besedovsky and del Rey perspective](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbi.2006.09.008)
supply context for neural, humoral, vascular and endocrine routes. Selected
passages are reviewed; underlying primary experiments remain separate research
work. These sources do not establish a universal stabilization rule, a clinical
depression mechanism or all proposed cross-level dependencies.

## Neurovascular experiments

[Longden et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4533) distinguishes isolated
capillary endothelial Kir2.1/Kcnj2 currents, upstream smooth-muscle voltage,
arteriole diameter, RBC flux and velocity, and relative cortical perfusion.
Focal capillary potassium evokes upstream responses; separating the vascular
preparation suppresses conducted dilation while direct arteriole stimulation
still works. Constitutive endothelial-targeted deletion reduces whisker
hyperemia with residual responses. It is not an adult-induced, capillary-only
intervention. Surface barium has additional vascular targets. The local diameter
null concerns sampled sites without pericytes and does not exclude pericyte
control elsewhere. Potassium ejection bypasses physiological release; the
complete cellular conduction path remains a testable interpretation.

[Noterman-Soulinthavong et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2615120123)
reports altered microvessel kinase proxies, endothelial Kir currents and vascular
responses after NAV-2729 treatment or partial inducible endothelial Arf6 loss.
The graph separates PIP2 pretreatment in isolated cells, concurrent treatment
in the pharmacological flow assay and supplementation in a separate genetic
cohort. Microvessel residual ATP and channel current do not directly measure
membrane PIP2. Myography measures diameter, not propagated voltage. Vehicle
changes the flow response, and concurrent NAV/PIP2 does not restore the higher
vehicle-treated response. These observations constrain a mechanism without
establishing exclusive mediation or treatment efficacy. Main text and figures
are reviewed; separate supporting information and raw data remain unread.

[Krolak et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2025.06.030) separates
inducible arterial endothelial Gja4/Gja5 deletion from capillary channel
perturbation. Aortic resistance/capacitance and neurobiotin assays support reduced
coupling in that tissue; retinal serotonin tracing retains loading-selection
limits. Awake cortical experiments distinguish directly imaged diameter from
optical HbT-related responses. Propagation speed is calculated from optical
onset timing, not measured blood-cell motion or cerebral endothelial voltage.
Local diving-arteriole and distant time-to-peak nulls remain separate records.

Connexin reporter zonation provides anatomical constraints, not a unique routing
rule. Postmortem vascular masks require registration and manual refinement;
thresholded neural activity and opsin expression define different distance
coordinates. These data can constrain graph construction only with explicit
measurement and perturbation semantics. The complete physiological pathway,
exclusive electrical carrier and minimal isoform arrangement remain unresolved.
Reading extent, nested samples, manuscript-version differences and unavailable
raw data/code are stated in the study and claim records.

The [Iadecola review](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2017.07.030) supplies
regional and vessel-segment context. The [Attwell and Iadecola abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12446129/)
is a limited conceptual source. [Roy and Sherrington](https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.1890.sp000321)
used brain-volume and pressure measurements and proposed local metabolic
regulation; the selected passages do not directly measure local firing-to-flow
coupling. None establishes a universal inverse map from perfusion or BOLD to
neural activity, a minimum carrier count or a unique physical graph geometry.

## Adult neurogenesis

The [Aimone review](https://doi.org/10.1152/physrev.00004.2014) and
[Kempermann, Song and Gage review](https://doi.org/10.1101/cshperspect.a018812)
supply scoped staging and functional terminology. All main scientific text and
figures are reviewed. Developmental milestones are represented as a definition;
they do not establish disjoint populations or a measured lineage. Pattern
separation has a separate measurement definition, and its comparison with
temporal-context and memory-resolution accounts remains unresolved.

The [Song experiment](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature11306) supports PV-dependent
suppression of RGL cycle markers, qualifying Aimone's conflicting attribution of
the effect direction. [Renzel's abstract](https://doi.org/10.1002/glia.22505)
distinguishes somatic and process receptors. [Suh's Introduction](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stem.2007.09.002)
identifies Sox2 as an HMG factor. Renzel remains a terminology check without an
admitted experiment; Suh's lineage assays are described below. Aimone's age-comparison wording remains
internally inconsistent, and the abbreviated Kempermann citation is nonunique.
Reading these reviews does not audit every cited primary protocol.

Song's main paper, Online Methods and supplementary Figures 1-8 are reviewed.
PV-evoked tonic currents are separate from five-day population cycling assays
and two-, seven- or thirty-day clone composition. PV activation lowers measured
EdU/MCM2 endpoints; inhibition increases them. Tested SST/VIP manipulations
retain null outcomes. Systemic diazepam uses distinct five-day population and
six-day clonal schedules. Conditional gamma2 deletion changes clone composition
without proving a unique founder mechanism or a mature-neuron production rate.

Figure 5c requires explicit comparator identity: PV activation lowers all three
cycle endpoints within isolated mice, yet isolated mice under light retain a
significant EdU excess over group-housed mice under light. The two MCM2 contrasts
are nonsignificant, which does not establish equivalence or complete rescue.
The etomidate dose, clone denominators and some fluorescent-tag labels remain
unresolved. Main text, Methods and Supplementary Figure 1 all print 200 mM for
focal GABA/muscimol; no unit correction is inferred. Animal-level data, actual
movie files and custom code were not replayed.

The proposed adaptive loop remains a hypothesis. Neither the circuit schematic
nor one reconstructed PV neuron's reported spatial coverage establishes a
closed functional feedback loop, quantitative edge weights or minimality.

[van Praag et al. (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1038/4151030a) supports excitability
and afferent input in selected birth-labeled mouse dentate neurons. Labeling
begins at approximately five to six weeks of age; slice recordings use runners
four to eight weeks later. Retroviral capture, neuronal markers, morphology,
BrdU counts and electrical responses measure different aspects of the process.
The graph retains the nonexcitable labeled population and comparison-specific
nulls. Functional output and memory necessity are not tested by these assays.

[Toni et al. (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2156) supplies selected
output recordings. Light stimulation of birth-labeled granule-cell populations
evokes postsynaptic currents, with glutamatergic blockade and a subset of
bicuculline-sensitive responses. The illustrated CA3 pyramidal response includes
an indirect inhibitory route. Light-to-response latency contains opsin-dependent
spike timing; it is not a directly measured synaptic delay or unique route length.
Selected recording yield is not a connection probability. Most recordings use
barium to increase excitability, and anatomical and functional samples have
different ages. Cross-sectional bouton profiles constrain recruitment hypotheses
without tracking replacement of the same connection.

[Sahay et al. (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature09817) separates the
Nestin-lineage Bax manipulation from direct control of neuron birth rate.
Surviving BrdU labels, DCX populations and selected dentate field-LTP responses
increase. Male mice show improved discrimination of similar contexts in the
main task; this is a behavioral proxy, not direct neural pattern separation or
general memory improvement. Other tasks and supplementary discrimination
variants have distinct nulls and interactions. Running-housed mice show greater
open-field exploration, with altered locomotion limiting an isolated anxiety
interpretation. Unique mediation by dentate neuron number remains unresolved.

The main discrimination figure and Methods differ in context order. The
supplementary irradiation comparison has overall treatment P=0.1 and interaction
P=0.3, despite late within-group differences and a conflicting F/P pair.
Regional irradiation and DCX depletion therefore do not establish selective
newborn-neuron cognitive necessity. These protocol and statistical discrepancies
remain scientific limitations in the current records. Both main papers and
supplements have been read to the stated extent; raw experiments and analysis
code have not been independently reproduced.

[Eriksson et al. (1998)](https://doi.org/10.1038/3305) supplies a distinct
human BrdU-labeling observation. The [author-uploaded full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/13476119_Neurogenesis_in_the_Adult_Human_Hippocampus)
and all five publisher figures were read. Five cancer patients, aged 57-72 at
death, supply different intervals after diagnostic labeling. Neuronal marker
colocalization, regional densities, SVZ marker-negative profiles and staining
controls have separate records. Reported marker fractions use BrdU-positive
cells as denominators; sampled densities cannot supply a neuron birth rate.

The uninfused control addresses staining background, not absence of neurogenesis.
Between-person intervals do not track one cohort's survival. SGZ boundaries
extend into the granule layer, and volume estimates use the microtome setting;
summing regional densities or treating section error bars as independent people
would misstate the measurement. The study leaves human functional integration
and cognitive significance untested. Label retention and morphology do not
selectively establish a complete division-to-memory mechanism. Raw optical
stacks, patient-level counts and clinical timing remain unverified.

## Adult macaque labeling and construction hypotheses

[Kornack and Rakic (1999)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC21935/)
reports BrdU labeling in ten adult rhesus and cynomolgus macaques. Regional
BrdU profiles, separate PCNA profiles, neuronal marker colabels, oligodendroglial
marker colabels and staining controls have distinct observational records.
The neuronal assay examines NeuN or TuJ1 at 32 days after five daily injections;
the illustrated O4/CNP assay uses 27 days after one injection. The astrocyte
claim refers to other studies, rather than a new GFAP assay in this experiment.

The methods do not allocate individual animals to every assay. Twenty sections
per neuronal marker cannot be counted as twenty animals. The maximum survival
is 72 days in Methods and 75 days in Results, and the longest retained-label
observation is not neuron-specific. Dye reversal and primary-antibody omission
address staining artifacts, without proving a clone or absence of toxicity.

The approximately 1,000 retained neuronal colabels per hippocampus and derived
0.004% per day depend on profile extrapolation, external anatomy and division by
five injections. Label coverage and subsequent survival remain entangled with
production. The cross-species comparison uses a separate mouse study and does
not identify an invariant biological rate or resolve absolute versus relative
age. The numerical estimate remains a conditional hypothesis.

Three other construction hypotheses remain separate: a shared multipotent
founder, an SGZ-to-GCL migration mechanism and population-preserving neuronal
replacement. Static phenotype, shape and location do not identify ancestry or
trajectories. Separate neuron and synapse censuses do not establish matched
birth/death balance, state-dependent feedback or replacement that preserves
memory. Each interpretation has a specified discriminating test.

All main text, Methods, figure captions and the three archived figure images
were reviewed. Animal-level assignments, raw counts and optical stacks remain
unverified. The separately reviewed Rakic (1985) and Eckenhoff–Rakic (1988)
protocols have different detection thresholds and sampling. Their numerical
results cannot be pooled without matched methods. The source card's learning,
network-formation and injury
necessities, weights and carrier minima are not established by this labeling
study. A complete birth-to-memory route remains unresolved.

recordings before a numerical replay. Source-code availability alone establishes
neither published-pipeline equivalence nor independent reproduction.

### Spatial gaze and fMRI

[Wang et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2018.10.061)
has a 44-adult behavioral experiment and a separate imaging experiment with
20 completers from 22 recruits. Usable scanner eye tracking is reported for
12 people; the independent functional localizer uses the same 20 imaging
participants. Main scientific text, Methods, Figures 1-5 and the complete
twelve-page [supplement](https://ars.els-cdn.com/content/image/1-s2.0-S1053811918320330-mmc1.pdf)
are reviewed. No specific public raw-data or custom-analysis deposit was
identified in the inspected publication records.

The anticipation index divides gaze-to-current-target distance at target onset
by previous-to-current-target distance. Lower values indicate more anticipation;
zero means gaze at the upcoming target. Gaze at the previous target gives one,
but that value does not uniquely locate the gaze.
It is not a percentage or a generally bounded probability. The 2022 review's
Figure 3C caption calls the reproduced index a percentage of anticipatory
movements, conflicting with this primary definition. A stationary one-point
control needs a denominator convention absent from the inspected methods.
Nearest-target accuracy and latency to enter an 80-pixel radius are separate
measurements.

Assigned description cost predicts average gaze error across selected templates.
Repeat and alternate advantages appear after several locations during the first
presentation. Boundaries in two-arcs and two-squares improve later; deep boundaries
retain nulls. The scanner subset has more extensive nulls than the separate
behavioral sample. These observations constrain possible sequence representations
without identifying a unique grammar. The one functional connection represents
the behavioral task comparison under its specified protocol.

BOLD associations with description cost and a gaze-derived boundary regressor
remain observational records. The latter uses position 5 minus the mean at 3 and
7, not a literal count of nested rules. Exclusive masking removes voxels with
detectable control effects; surviving voxels do not demonstrate independence.
The direct supplementary comparisons use different thresholds and a different
memory contrast. Task-selected ROI plots also differ from the independently
localized sentence/calculation assays. Generally weak language ROI responses
and limited map overlap do not establish language-circuit necessity or complete
silence. Similarity changes in selected ROIs do not recover the joint program.

Quantitative use requires participant-level reconciliation of scanner gaze
membership, run counts, slope sign, statistical pairs, contrast weights and RSA
group membership. Sequence names also require exact stimulus and parse identity:
the 2019 alternate uses two-step and opposing one-step rotations, whereas the
2021 MEG alternate uses three-step and opposing one-step rotations. The studies
assign different nesting labels to two-crosses. Neither nominal agreement nor
anatomical ordering supplies a causal construction rule.

### Binary sound sequences and compression

[Al Roumi, Planton et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.84376)
is the published binary-sound fMRI/MEG study. The scientific text, Methods,
eight main figures, seven figure supplements, main Table 1, supplementary
files 1-3 and reporting checklist are reviewed. All figures, Table 1 and the
embedded program table are visually inspected. The 2022 Opinion proof attributes
its binary imaging to unpublished work; exact reuse of every panel and cohort
has not been established. The final article byline starts with Al Roumi,
with Al Roumi and Planton sharing first authorship.

The graph separates three contexts: 23 adults for detection behavior,
22 of those people for imaging after one motion exclusion, and 19 separate
adults for MEG. The fMRI task has ten templates and requires detection responses;
MEG has seven templates and attentive listening without an online button.
Both present sixteen 50-ms tones at 250-ms onset intervals, with ten habituation
sequences per template/version. Test blocks differ: fMRI has eighteen sequences
with six violations; MEG has thirty-six with twenty-four violations. The same
participants subsequently bracket visual sequences after optional auditory
re-exposure. Seven bracketing templates pool 42 people; three use only 23.

The binary language defines stay, switch, repetition and nested composition.
Its constrained model preserves runs of identical items. Assigned description
scores predict detection sensitivity and correct-response latency. Among the
twelve reported fits, the language plus first-order surprise leads the tested
alternatives. This is a comparison on selected templates, not prediction of new
grammars or proof of a unique mental representation. The response-time prose
assigns AIC 610.8 to its third-ranked model while reporting 3031.2 for the best;
the inconsistent number is not used as a corrected quantitative result.
The false-alarm slope is nonsignificant in the group-mean analysis but positive
in the participant-intercept model. Both findings retain their analysis scope.

Bracketing agrees with the proposed parse for nine templates. Alternate is an
explicit exception: the reported correlation is r=0.08, P=0.77, and participants
favor AB pairs. Visual grouping and a nonminimum auditory parse remain competing
explanations. The language correlation includes outer boundaries while the
transition correlation omits them, so these are not matched-dimensional model
tests. The printed repetition penalty `ceil(log10 n)` is not the decimal digit
count at powers of ten or at one. Exact grammar semantics and shortest-program
claims require an executable specification; assigned costs do not measure
physical resources or supply canonical generative rules.

During habituation, fMRI complexity associations include frontal, parietal,
temporal and cerebellar regions. Standard-trial positive effects occupy a
narrower network. Deviant effects are predominantly negative; hit-only controls
retain corrected negative effects in four of nine task ROIs, with five nulls.
The nine ROIs use group-defined search spheres before within-person A/B voxel
selection, leaving acknowledged circularity. Hit-only selection does not remove
all response and selection confounding. Most language-localizer responses are
weak, but left IFG pars opercularis and posterior STS have specified effects.
Mathematics-localizer overlap does not establish necessity, and weak language
overlap does not establish dispensability. This localizer uses a different
language contrast from Wang et al. (2019).

The standard-trial figure reference and cerebellar labels conflict across text,
figures and tables. Main Table 1 prints positive coordinate magnitudes for
left-hemisphere entries, unlike signed coordinates in Methods. Exact anatomical
coordinates are not silently repaired or used to construct connections.
The reported BOLD time courses are resampled block summaries, not millisecond
measurements of neural processing order.

MEG global field power has positive habituation and negative deviant complexity
associations, with a standard-trial null. Sensor regressions do find standard
effects, including after the specified transition-statistics controls. These
different readouts must not be merged. The transition observer does not exhaust
ordinal, chunking or finite-state alternatives. Source estimates lack the
expected anterior prefrontal effect; baseline subtraction and measurement
sensitivity limit an absence claim. The printed 206-gradiometer count conflicts
with the stated 306-channel instrument's 102 magnetometers and 204 gradiometers.
An impossible `p<0.000` figure label and minor interval differences require
reconciliation before numerical replay.

The MEG classifier distinguishes standard and deviant labels. Its average
readout is nonsignificant for Shrinking and Complex, while position 15 has a
significant violation signal in both. Neither averaged nulls nor position
effects uniquely determine a parse. Decision-vector projections are distinct
from classification accuracy and calibrated probabilities. Temporal
generalization within familiar templates does not decode a complete program
or establish a directed prefrontal-to-auditory construction pathway.

The publication links [fMRI data](https://openneuro.org/datasets/ds004482/versions/1.0.0)
and [MEG data](https://openneuro.org/datasets/ds004483/versions/1.0.0).
Version 1.0.0 metadata and recursive file inventories are inspected. fMRI has
23 participant rows and directories, without identifying the imaging exclusion.
MEG has nineteen participant directories, but only fourteen participant-table
rows, for sub-06 through sub-19. Recording paths exist for the first five too;
the finding concerns missing metadata, not five absent recordings. Raw annex
files have not been downloaded or analyzed.

Selected [MEG source](https://github.com/Fosca/Binary_Sequences/tree/9528a5a749f707861aff91eedc3fe9f05c806ca3)
uses a fitted StandardScaler/linear-SVC pipeline and two reversed folds.
Four-fold docstrings and leave-one-run-out wording do not describe those loops.
The single-run fallback shuffles item epochs within a run; the configuration
omits two runs for sub-03 and one for sub-07. Consequently, complete A/B transfer
cannot be assumed for every fold. Saved indices, possible shared-sequence
contamination, sensor variants and figure call paths need examination.
The inspected [stimulus helper](https://github.com/sam-planton/ABseqfMRI_scripts/blob/ef5f4ea6e4433cfc83a817452a24b30ffe022cf2/ExperimentScripts_%26_Stimuli/utils/sequences_and_violations_list.m)
contains seven templates despite an fMRI comment; it is not a verified
implementation of the ten-template imaging task. Its Pairs&Alt.1 positions
